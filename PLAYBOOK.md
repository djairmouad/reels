# Reel Playbook — @mouad.___.dev
Living knowledge base, updated daily by the research task (cloud). The video task reads it before writing each day's 3 reels.
Goal: views + web-development clients (small business owners worldwide, English).
Hard rules: no "free" offers; strictly flat 2D characters in every video; no fake clients/results/testimonials; stats only with a real source.

## 1. Algorithm (what Instagram rewards now)
- Top signals (Mosseri): sends per reach (DM shares) → likes per reach → watch time. Sends matter most for reaching non-followers; in Oct 2026 Q&As he said Instagram weighs reels people send to a friend far more than comments. Saves count too.
- Every reel should give a reason to send it to someone ("send this to the person who runs your website").
- Hook decides everything: Instagram's own **skip rate** = % of views that leave before 3 s. Treat it as the #1 health metric for each reel.
- **Retention chart** (per reel, in Insights): Instagram says the flatter the line, the more engaged the audience. Find the second where it drops and fix that beat in the next reels. Instagram's own example: a drop at ~4 s means the hook needs work.
- Skip-rate benchmark: no official one. Socialinsider (9.8M organic reels, Jan–Jun 2026) averages ≈65% for 1–5K-follower accounts, ≈60% for 100K–1M. Our target: < 50%; anything > 60% = rewrite the hook type.
- Length: reels up to 3 min are recommendable to non-followers; **never go over 3:00** (Buffer, 2.7M reels Jan–Aug 2026: 180 s+ reels got −49% reach vs the account's own baseline). Under 90 s every length bucket was within ~3% of baseline (Buffer) and Socialinsider saw only 5.6% vs 5.2% reach rate for 30–60 s vs < 30 s → length is not our lever, retention is. Keep 18–35 s while our avg watch is ~5–12 s; make a reel longer only when the story needs it (§7).
- **Originality (account-level, Apr 2026):** accounts that mostly post unoriginal content (reposts, reuploads, light edits) lose recommendations to non-followers; eligibility returns once most posts in a rolling 30 days are original. Borders, watermarks, speed changes or credits do not make a repost original. Our own animation + voice-over is original — never repost other creators' clips; share others' content to Stories only. Check Settings → Account Status if reach drops suddenly.
- **Content protection (Nov 17, 2026, reported):** creators are getting a tool that finds copies of their Reels and lets them block the copy, add an "original by" label, or track it. If Mouad gets the activity-feed notification, enrol (covers every new reel). Instagram also says it is looking at limiting reach of accounts that keep AI-reposting others' work.
- Benchmarks (Metricool 2026 Instagram study): average Reels watch time ≈ 8.5 s; ~65% of a reel's views come in its first 3 days → judge a reel after 72 h, not 16 h.
- No other-app watermarks (CapCut/TikTok logos): content made in other apps is fine only without the watermark (Mosseri, Oct 2026).
- AI: content is not demoted for being AI (Mosseri). The "AI-generated profile" label (Aug 2026) is only for accounts featuring AI-generated *people*; using AI for graphics/edits/captions doesn't need it. Our strictly flat 2D characters avoid this — never use realistic AI humans.
- Scheduling posts does not hurt reach; deleting a post doesn't hurt other posts (Mosseri, Oct 2026).
- Trial reels (shown to non-followers first; if the account has access): several per day is fine, but re-posting the *same* reel as a trial again and again will be cracked down on. A daily cap is being tested.
- Reels "series" linking was removed (Oct 28, 2026): don't rely on linked Part 1/Part 2; every reel must stand alone.
- "Your Algorithm" lets viewers add/remove topics for their Reels feed: keep every reel clearly about one topic (websites for small businesses) in voice, on-screen text and caption keywords.
Sources: socialinsider.io (Reels statistics 2026, updated Nov 6), buffer.com (Reels length study, 2.7M reels, Nov 12 2026), socialmediatoday.com (Instagram Edits video tips, Aug 2026), creators.instagram.com (original content guidelines), tubefilter.com (aggregator penalty, Apr 2026), techcrunch.com (AI-generated profile label, Aug 2026), socialsamosa.com + metricool.com (skip rate & retention chart), zoomsphere.com (Mosseri Q&As Oct 2026, secondhand), metricool.com (Instagram news: series removal; 2026 Instagram study via newsletter, Jun 2026), thenextweb.com (content protection + AI-repost limits, Nov 17 2026), sproutsocial.com (algorithm 2026).

## 2. Video types that work for web devs / agencies
Every format must start from an owner's problem a customer feels (a lost order/booking/call). Ranked by our own data (§6b) + research:
1. **Customer-journey story** (our only < 50% skip so far): a scene + a loss → customer tries the site on the phone → fails → fixed site → succeeds. Use for ≥ 1 of 3 daily reels.
2. **Before → after with a customer**: the bad site *as a customer uses it* → 3–4 named fixes → the action works. Never a bare build/showcase: "3 websites, 3 businesses" (82%) and the florist 4-step demo (74–77%) flopped.
3. **"The most common mistake on [niche] websites"** (web-designer idea lists, 2026): one mistake, shown on a phone mockup, one fix. Only say "I checked 20 [niche] sites" if Mouad really did.
4. **Website breakdown of an invented concept site**: 3 problems circled on the phone in 20–25 s, labelled "concept" (never a real business, never a fake client).
5. **Myth vs fact with a real stat** — "Instagram is enough for my shop." → DreamHost 2026 Local Business Trust Index (1,201 US adults, surveyed Nov 2025): 39% have skipped visiting/buying from a business because it had no website; 58% check a business's website to confirm what they saw on social, Google or AI tools; 60% say an outdated-looking site hurts their trust. Show the source on screen ("Source: DreamHost survey, 2025").
6. **Recurring named series** (repeatable formats beat one-off trends, ManyChat 2026): same title card style + number each time, e.g. "Website ER #4: the bakery that hid its menu". Series linking is gone (§1), so each episode must stand alone.
7. **Price/value breakdown**: "What a website for a café actually includes" — each part tied to a customer action (menu, order button, map, reviews). Never "free".
8. **Seasonal angle (late Nov–Dec)**: holiday rush scenes — "Christmas cake orders at 11 pm: can they order from your site?" Holiday/Black Friday formats are trending now (New Engen, Nov 13 2026); drop it after the holidays.
Avoid: dance/choreo and trending-meme formats (wrong audience, can't do with our style), pure portfolio montages, tool/Figma process reels (other devs watch, owners don't).
- **Reality check on clients:** web designer Marie Brown (Josh Hall podcast ep. 392, 2025) says few clients name Instagram as where they found her; the usual path is meet/hear of her → see her posts for months → hire. Reels build trust over time: keep one clear promise across all posts and ask every lead "how did you find me?" (§6).
- **Reels = reach, carousels = depth** (Buffer, 4M+ posts): Reels got 1.36× the reach of carousels, but carousels earned ~2× engagement per person reached. Reels stay our engine; see §7 for a carousel test.
Sources: dreamhost.com (2026 Local Business Trust Index), socialmon.ai (web-designer post ideas, May 2026), manychat.com (2026 creator trends: repeatable series), newengen.com (Instagram trends Nov 2026), joshhall.co (ep. 392 Marie Brown), buffer.com (best content format, Mar 2026).

## 3. Hooks
Formula: call out the viewer + pain + visual change in the first second. Text on screen within 1 s (in frame 1 if possible).
- **3 layers in frame 1:** (a) visual already moving/changing, (b) on-screen text 5–10 words, (c) spoken line. Text can add tension to the voice instead of repeating it (voice: "Same pizza." / text: "Why is one shop empty?").
- **Two-step test for every hook:** instant clarity first (who it's for + the pain/outcome), curiosity gap second. Promise first, tease second. Simple words (a 10-year-old understands it).
- Instagram's own advice: open on a bold statement, a surprising visual, or a question that demands an answer; cut anything that slows the start (no title card).
- **Hook decay:** templates stop working once a niche copies them. Never use the same hook *type* in 2 consecutive reels; across each day's 3 reels use 3 different types and log type + skip rate in §6b.
- Hook types to rotate (with lines):
  - **Contrast / side-by-side** (our best so far, §6b — but only when it shows a *physical scene + a loss*: full vs empty shop; "One website wins every round" was abstract and got 78% skip): "Same street. Same pizza. One is full, one is empty." · "Two bakeries. Same prices. Only one gets orders at night." · "Two salons on one street. One phone keeps ringing."
  - **Numbered list:** "3 signs your website is costing you clients." · "3 things every café website needs. Most have zero."
  - **Mid-action story:** "She wanted to order a cake. The website kept loading." · "He tried to book a table at 11 pm. Nobody answered."
  - **Direct callout:** "Dentists: your website is losing patients while you sleep." · "Owners of [niche]: send this to whoever made your website." (built for DM sends)
  - **Curiosity:** "The thing no one tells restaurant owners about their website." · "This one button decides if a customer calls you."
  - **Contrarian:** "Stop posting more on Instagram. Fix this first." · "Your website isn't ugly. That's not the problem."
  - **Metaphor / what-if:** "What if your website was an employee?" · "Your website is your front desk." (weakest on our account so far: open on the bad result, not the metaphor)
  - **Visual threat:** "If your website looks like this, it's sending customers to your competitor."
Sources: later.com (scroll-stopping hook types, Apr 2026), membership.io (hook decay + test 3 variants, Sep 2026), truefuturemedia.com (two-step hook test), refunnel.beehiiv.com (frame-1 text/voice tension, Apr 2026), viral.app (numbered-list openings broke out most, 150K cross-posts, Oct 2026), socialmediatoday.com (Instagram Edits tips, Aug 2026).

## 3b. Re-hooks (Mouad's rule: every reel has one)
A re-hook is a second hook placed where viewers typically swipe (≈35–60% of the reel) that re-opens curiosity.
- Lines: "But the worst part is this." · "And it gets worse." · "Number three is the one costing you the most." ·
  "Wait — watch what happens when they try to book." · "This next one kills most small businesses." · "Here's the part nobody tells you."
- Pair it with a visual pattern interrupt (punch zoom, big word, sound hit) so it works muted too.
- Rotate lines; never repeat the same re-hook in consecutive videos. Research task: add new proven re-hook lines here.

## 4. Script structure (18–35 s)
Every reel = **Hook → Bridge → Body → Payoff → CTA (≤ 2 s) → loop**. Pick ONE body pattern per reel and rotate across the day's 3 reels:
- **A. Story arc (customer journey)** — our best performer so far (pizza, §6b). Beats:
  0–2 s hook = a concrete scene + a loss ("Same street. Same pizza. One is full, one is empty.") →
  2–4 s bridge ("Here's what the customer saw.") → 4–12 s conflict: one customer tries to order/book/call, 2 things fail on the phone mockup →
  ~45% re-hook (§3b) → 12–20 s turn: same customer, same phone, the fixed site → 20–26 s payoff: the action succeeds on screen (order placed, table booked) → CTA → last line points back to the opening scene.
- **B. Numbered list** — "3 signs…": number on screen for each point ("2/3"), one problem + one fix per point, save the strongest for last and say so ("Number 3 costs the most").
- **C. Demo / before → after** — bad site → 3–4 quick fixes, each named in ≤ 4 words → finished site. Always tie it to a customer's problem first; a showcase with no problem flopped (82% skip, §6b). Label redesigns "concept" — never imply a real client.
Rules:
- **Voice from the first second**: the spoken hook starts at 0:00, no music-only intro (Emplifi, 10,110 Facebook Reels, Mar 2026: speech in the first 3 s gave ~25% higher 10-s retention vs music only; Facebook data, but our reels are voice-led anyway).
- **Bridge line** (1 short sentence) between hook and body so the hook's promise flows into proof: "Here's what she saw." · "Watch what happens next." · "Here's why." · "Look at the phone."
- **Payoff before CTA**: the CTA only comes after the payoff, max 2 s, then end. CTA line: "Contact us, and we'll update your website. Comment website to get started." (vary wording slightly; keep the keyword WEBSITE).
- **Loop ending**: write the last line so it leads back into the first frame (e.g. end "…so tomorrow, the street looks like this again" over the opening shot). Rewatches add watch time; Emplifi found loops raised replays (+18.7%) on very short reels (≤ 7 s), so treat longer loops as a test.
- **Plain words** (a 10-year-old understands): no "SEO", "UX", "navigation", "responsive", "conversion" — say "shows up on Google", "easy to use", "menu", "works on phones", "more orders". Web designer Blaze Smith (grew to ~12K IG followers in a year, Josh Hall podcast) scripts at a 5th-grade level and rewrites a hook dozens of times; the 2nd line matters as much as the 1st.
- One idea per sentence, ≤ 12 words per line, no intro. Never > 3 s of voice without a visual change.
- **Self-check before rendering**: "Would a café/dentist/gym owner watching this think: this is the person I want to build my site?" If the reel is only clever, not useful to an owner, rewrite.
- **Mix**: most reels give value (a problem + its fix the owner can understand); about 1 in 3 can lead with the offer, but still wrapped in a problem story (service-business creator Lucy Bedewi on Buffer: ~70% value / 30% promo, DM-keyword CTA instead of link in bio).
Sources: socialmediatoday.com + emplifi.io (Reels speech/loop data, Mar 2026), socialync.io (hook-body-payoff structure, Mar 2026), buzzsprout.com (Josh Hall podcast ep. 374 with Blaze Smith, Apr 2025), buffer.com (six-figure service business via Reels, 2023).

## 5. Editing
Instagram's own 5-step editing advice (Aug 2026): hook in the first seconds and trim anything slow at the start → cut on the beat and match transitions to the music's energy → on-screen text for sound-off viewers, graphics/effects used sparingly → check skip rate + drop-off in Insights → reuse templates. Our rules:
- **Frame 1:** movement already happening; no static opening, no logo, no title card. The problem must be visible and spoken before 0:03.
- **Character in the first 3 s:** show the flat 2D character (owner or customer) reacting within 0–3 s, next to or holding the phone mockup. Emplifi (Facebook brand Reels, Mar 2026): a person visible in the first 3 s gave +10% 10-s retention; ours is a 2D character, so treat as a test (§7), never a realistic human.
- **Full-frame vertical 9:16 (1080×1920)**, no letterbox/black bars (Emplifi: vertical vs other formats +23% 10-s retention, +21% reach).
- **Captions:** burned-in word-by-word, max ~6 words on screen, ≥ 45 px bold sans-serif with dark stroke/shadow, key word in colour. Text on screen also lifted retention in Emplifi's data (+5% at 3 s, +8% at 10 s).
- **Safe zones (1080×1920):** nothing important in the bottom ~280 px (caption/audio bar), right ~100 px (like/share icons) or top ~150 px. Hook text in the band ~200–600 px from the top; word-by-word captions in the middle band (~900–1450 px). Phone mockup centred, its key UI (buttons, "Order" bar) above the bottom 280 px. (Vendor guidance, not Meta — check on a real phone preview.)
- **Pacing — clarity over frantic cuts:** a visual change (cut, zoom, new element, highlight on the phone) about every 2–3 s and never > 3 s without one; don't cut every second just for speed. Emplifi found overall pacing had little effect on retention and slower pacing slightly *helped* 30-s retention (+8%). Speed up only at the hook and the re-hook.
- **Cut on the beat:** land scene changes and caption pops on music beats; sound effect (whoosh, pop, ding, error buzz) on every visual beat, a distinct "fail" sound when the bad site breaks and a "success" sound at the payoff.
- **Mid-reel dip fix:** retention usually sags in the middle → put the re-hook (§3b) there with a punch-zoom + big word + sound hit.
- **Audio:** our own voice-over leads from 0:00; quiet music bed under it, total loudness ≈ -14 LUFS. Business accounts may only use commercially licensed audio from Instagram's library — use our own music bed or the commercial library; check a trending sound is available to the account before using it (New Engen, Nov 2026). Never reuse another creator's original audio over new visuals (Instagram is testing flags for AI videos that do this, Nov 2026).
- Progress bar at top (inside the top safe zone, thin). Export clean: no editor watermarks/end cards.
- Optional Edits tools: beat markers (cut on beat), "First Draft" auto-trims pauses, Mac app with keyframes + auto-captions + 4K no-watermark export.
Sources: socialmediatoday.com (Instagram's creator video editing tips, Aug 2026; Edits updates Jan 2026), emplifi.io (Facebook Reels data: person/text/pacing/vertical, Mar 2026), trymypost.com (Reels safe zones, 2026), metricool.com (Edits/Instagram news, Nov 2026), thenextweb.com (content protection + AI audio reuse, Nov 17 2026).

## 6. Posting & growth
- Consistency beats volume; 3 reels/day is our cadence.
- Slots (Africa/Lagos): 14:00, 18:00, 01:00 → re-check against our own insights weekly.
- Caption: first line repeats the hook, 3–5 niche hashtags, comment keyword "WEBSITE".
- Reply to every comment fast (early engagement helps distribution); DM everyone who comments "WEBSITE".
- Scheduling is safe (no reach penalty), so queue all 3 daily reels in advance.
- Asking for a comment works: Metricool (24M posts, 2026) saw comment-asking CTAs roughly triple comments, while asking for likes slightly lowered likes → keep the "Comment WEBSITE" CTA, never "like this".
- Ask every person who DMs or books "How did you find me?" and note it — that is the real client metric, not views (§2 reality check).

## 7. Ideas to test next
- POV format, myth-vs-fact, "send this to your web designer" hooks, niches: gym, real estate, restaurant, auto repair, florist.
- A/B two hooks for the same script via trial reels (if available): different first 3 s, compare skip rate.
- Frame 1 = phone mockup alone vs. phone + reacting 2D character in the same frame (Emplifi: person in first 3 s helped 10-s retention) — compare skip rate.
- Calm pacing (visual change every ~2.5 s, fast only at hook/re-hook) vs. a change every ~1 s, same script — compare avg watch time.
- Cut-on-beat edit (scene changes on music beats) vs. cuts placed only on voice lines — compare avg watch.
- Contrast hook in new niches (2 shops/2 phones split-screen: gym, barber, florist) to check the pizza result wasn't a fluke.
- Numbered-list hook as on-screen text ("3 things every [niche] website needs") vs. contrast hook — skip rate + saves.
- One 30–45 s story reel per day vs. 15–25 s — compare reach and avg watch %.
- Loop ending (last line flows into frame 1) vs. a hard stop after the CTA — compare avg watch time (can exceed 100% of length if people rewatch).
- Story arc with a named customer moment ("She tried to book at 11 pm") vs. the same script told as a 3-point list — skip rate + avg watch.
- "Website breakdown" format: a generic (unnamed, invented) local-business site, 3 problems circled on the phone mockup in 20–25 s; labelled "concept". Idea from web designer Blaze Smith, who got clients by breaking down well-known sites.
- Myth-vs-fact reel with the DreamHost stat on screen ("39% skipped a business with no website") vs a story reel the same day — skip rate + shares (stats may be more sendable).
- Named series ("Website ER #1, #2…"): run 5 episodes, same opening style; compare avg skip rate vs non-series reels that week.
- Holiday-rush story reels (late Nov–Dec): 1 per day for a week vs evergreen stories.
- (For Mouad, outside the reel task) 1 carousel per week that turns the best reel's 3 points into slides, for followers' saves (Buffer: carousels ~2× engagement per reach).
- Hashtags: Metricool's 2026 study found posts with hashtags got ~31% fewer views (correlation only). Test 0 vs our 3–5 niche tags for a week; Saturday research to confirm.
